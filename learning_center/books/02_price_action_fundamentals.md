# Price Action Trading — Fundamentals

## ملخص سريع (عربي)
تحليل الأكشن السعري يعتمد على قراءة الشموع والهيكل السعري مباشرة دون مؤشرات مساعدة. الأساس: تحديد الدعم والمقاومة، قراءة شكل الشموع (ابتلاعية، مطرقة، دوجي) كدليل على تغيّر توازن القوة بين المشترين والبائعين، وربط ذلك بمناطق الاختراق وإعادة الاختبار (Breakout & Retest).

---

## 1. Support & Resistance (الدعم والمقاومة)
- **Support:** a price level where buying pressure has historically been strong enough to halt or reverse a decline.
- **Resistance:** a price level where selling pressure has historically halted or reversed an advance.
- Levels gain significance with (a) number of prior touches, (b) recency, (c) reaction strength (how fast/far price moved away after touching it).
- **Role reversal:** once broken with a decisive close, resistance often becomes support (and vice versa) — this is one of the most reliable price-action observations.

## 2. Key Candlestick Patterns
- **Bullish/Bearish Engulfing:** a candle whose body fully covers the prior candle's body, in the opposite direction — signals a potential shift in short-term control.
- **Pin Bar / Hammer / Shooting Star:** a candle with a small body and a long wick on one side, showing price was pushed strongly one way and then rejected — often marks exhaustion at a level.
- **Doji:** open and close nearly equal, signaling indecision; significance depends heavily on where it forms (e.g., a doji at a well-tested resistance carries more weight than one in the middle of a range).
- **Inside Bar:** a candle whose entire range sits inside the prior candle's range — represents contraction/consolidation, often preceding a breakout.

## 3. Chart Patterns
- **Double Top / Double Bottom:** two swing extremes at a similar level with a pullback between them — suggests supply/demand exhaustion at that level.
- **Head & Shoulders / Inverse Head & Shoulders:** three-peak (or trough) structure with the middle one most extreme — a classic reversal pattern, confirmed on a break of the "neckline."
- **Ascending/Descending Triangle:** a flat level on one side with a sloping trendline on the other, showing gradual pressure building toward a breakout.
- **Flags & Pennants:** short consolidations after a sharp move, often resolving in the direction of the original move (continuation patterns).

## 4. Trendlines & Channels
- A trendline connects at least two (ideally three+) swing points; more touches and more time elapsed generally increase (but never guarantee) its significance.
- A break of a well-respected trendline is a warning sign of a structure shift, but should ideally be confirmed with a candle close beyond it, not just an intrabar poke.
- Parallel channels (trendline + parallel line through opposite swings) help visualize the range of an established trend and flag potential reversal zones at channel edges.

## 5. Breakout & Retest
- A **breakout** occurs when price closes decisively beyond a well-established support/resistance or trendline.
- A **retest** is when price returns to that broken level shortly after, testing whether it now holds as the opposite role (old resistance as new support, etc.).
- Entering on the retest (rather than the initial breakout candle) generally offers a tighter, more defined risk level, at the cost of sometimes missing fast-moving breakouts that never look back.
- **False breakout (fakeout):** price briefly breaks a level then reverses back inside — common around obvious levels where many traders have placed orders; watching for a quick close back inside the range is a warning sign of this.

## 6. Volume & Momentum Context (When Available)
- Rising volume on a breakout adds confidence that the move has real participation behind it; a breakout on very light volume is more prone to failing.
- Momentum divergence (price making a new extreme while a momentum reading fails to confirm it) can flag weakening conviction behind a move, though it is a lagging/context tool rather than a standalone signal.

## 7. Combining Price Action Signals
1. Establish the higher-timeframe trend and the nearest significant support/resistance levels.
2. Wait for a recognizable candlestick or chart pattern at that level.
3. Prefer confluence: a pin bar at a well-tested trendline is stronger evidence than either alone.
4. Use the pattern's own structure to define risk (e.g., stop beyond a pin bar's wick, beyond a double-bottom's low).

## Important Caveat
Price-action patterns describe historical tendencies in how markets have behaved, not fixed rules the market must obey. Any pattern can fail, and failure itself is useful information (often signaling that the opposing side is in stronger control than the pattern suggested).
